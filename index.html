About Us

An airplane is a heavy-weight aircraft that flies through the air using four primary aerodynamic forces: lift, thrust, drag, and weight. When an airplane speeds down the runway, the unique curved shape of its wings (known as an airfoil) generates lift, pushing the aircraft upward. At the same time, powerful jet engines create thrust, which drives the airplane forward through the skies. Opposing these movements are drag (air resistance slowing the plane down) and weight (the force of gravity pulling it toward the Earth). An airplane achieves stable flight when its thrust overcomes drag and its lift overcomes weight. To withstand extreme air pressure while remaining fuel-efficient, modern aircraft are built using lightweight yet incredibly durable materials like aluminum and carbon fiber composites.
